Professional traders don't buy because "the RSI is oversold" or "the chart looks good." They make decisions by weighing multiple factors that increase or decrease the probability of a profitable trade. Think of it as a decision matrix rather than a single signal.

Here's a practical framework of the factors professionals consider.

## 1. Market Regime (Highest Priority)

The first question is: **What kind of market am I trading?**

- Trending up
- Trending down
- Range-bound
- High volatility
- Low volatility
- Bull market
- Bear market

A strategy that works in a trending market often fails in a sideways market.

Metrics:

- 50 EMA vs 200 EMA
- ADX (trend strength)
- Average True Range (ATR)
- Volatility Index (e.g., India VIX)

---

## 2. Market Structure

Professionals identify where price is within the larger structure.

They look for:

- Higher highs and higher lows
- Lower highs and lower lows
- Support and resistance
- Swing highs/lows
- Trendlines
- Channels

Question:

> Is the stock making a continuation move or a reversal?

---

## 3. Relative Strength

A stock can be strong even if the market is weak.

Professionals compare:

- Stock vs Nifty 50
- Stock vs sector index
- Stock vs peers

Example:

Nifty +0.5%

Stock +4%

That stock is showing relative strength.

---

## 4. Volume

Price without volume is less convincing.

Key questions:

- Is volume above average?
- Is volume confirming the breakout?
- Is volume declining during pullbacks?
- Is institutional buying evident?

Watch:

- Volume spikes
- Delivery percentage (important in Indian markets)
- Volume Profile
- VWAP

---

## 5. Liquidity

Professionals avoid stocks with poor liquidity because they can be difficult to enter or exit efficiently.

They assess:

- Average daily traded value
- Bid-ask spread
- Market depth
- Order book imbalance

---

## 6. Trend Alignment Across Timeframes

They don't trade based on one chart alone.

Typical process:

- Weekly: long-term trend
- Daily: intermediate trend
- Hourly or 15-minute: entry

For example:

- Weekly uptrend
- Daily pullback
- Hourly bullish reversal

This alignment often improves trade quality.

---

## 7. Volatility

Volatility influences both opportunity and risk.

Measures:

- ATR
- India VIX
- Historical volatility
- Implied volatility (especially for options)

Professionals adjust stop-losses and position sizes based on volatility rather than using fixed distances.

---

## 8. Risk-to-Reward

Before entering, many traders ask:

- If I'm right, how much can I make?
- If I'm wrong, how much will I lose?

Common minimum target:

- 1:2 or 1:3 reward-to-risk

A trade with limited upside relative to the risk is often skipped.

---

## 9. Position Sizing

Professionals don't take the same size on every trade.

Factors include:

- Account size
- Volatility
- Stop-loss distance
- Maximum acceptable portfolio risk
- Correlation with existing positions

---

## 10. Sector Rotation

Money tends to move between sectors.

For example:

- Banks
- IT
- Pharma
- Auto
- Metals
- FMCG

A stock often has a better chance of outperforming when its sector is also attracting capital.

---

## 11. Institutional Activity

Large investors can significantly influence prices.

Professionals monitor:

- Foreign Institutional Investor (FII) flows
- Domestic Institutional Investor (DII) flows
- Mutual fund activity
- Bulk deals
- Block deals
- Insider buying/selling
- Promoter shareholding changes

---

## 12. Fundamental Catalysts

Even short-term traders pay attention to scheduled events.

Examples:

- Earnings
- Guidance revisions
- Dividends
- Mergers
- Management changes
- New products
- Regulatory decisions
- Government policy announcements

These events can create sharp price moves.

---

## 13. Macroeconomic Factors

Markets respond to broader economic conditions.

Examples:

- Interest rate decisions
- Inflation
- GDP growth
- Employment data
- Currency movements
- Crude oil prices
- Bond yields

In India, many traders closely follow announcements from the Reserve Bank of India because policy changes can affect market sentiment.

---

## 14. Market Breadth

A rising index isn't always a healthy market.

Professionals look at:

- Advance/Decline ratio
- New highs vs new lows
- Percentage of stocks above the 50-day moving average
- Sector participation

Strong breadth can confirm a trend.

---

## 15. Sentiment

Sentiment helps gauge whether optimism or fear is becoming extreme.

Indicators include:

- Put/Call ratio
- Volatility indices
- Retail positioning
- News sentiment
- Social media activity (used cautiously)

---

## 16. Options Data

Options can reveal where market participants are concentrated.

Common metrics:

- Open Interest (OI)
- Changes in OI
- Put/Call ratio
- Gamma exposure
- Max Pain (used with caution)
- Implied volatility skew

---

## 17. Statistical Edge

Many professionals validate their ideas with historical data.

Questions include:

- Has this setup worked over hundreds of past trades?
- What is the win rate?
- Average profit?
- Average loss?
- Maximum drawdown?
- Expectancy?

A strategy should be supported by evidence, not just intuition.

---

## 18. Trade Execution

A good idea can fail due to poor execution.

Professionals consider:

- Market orders vs limit orders
- Slippage
- Trading costs
- Partial fills
- Timing around market open and close

---

## 19. Correlation

Avoid concentrating risk unintentionally.

Examples:

- Buying three bank stocks may create one large sector bet.
- Holding multiple highly correlated stocks reduces diversification.

Many traders monitor portfolio exposure rather than just individual trades.

---

## 20. Trading Psychology

Even a strong strategy can underperform if executed inconsistently.

Professionals work on:

- Following predefined rules
- Avoiding revenge trading
- Managing fear and greed
- Staying patient
- Keeping a trading journal
- Reviewing performance regularly

---

### A practical decision framework

Before entering a trade, a professional might mentally score it like this:

| Factor            | Example Question                                     |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Market regime     | Is the overall market supportive?                    |
| Trend             | Is the primary trend up or down?                     |
| Market structure  | Is price near a meaningful support or resistance?    |
| Relative strength | Is this stock outperforming its index and sector?    |
| Volume            | Does volume confirm the move?                        |
| Volatility        | Is the stop-loss appropriate for current volatility? |
| Catalyst          | Is there a news or earnings event coming?            |
| Risk/Reward       | Is the potential reward at least twice the risk?     |
| Position size     | Does this fit my risk limits?                        |
| Execution         | Can I enter without excessive slippage?              |
